# One introduction from ten thousand people

The last piece ended on a habit: be the one who knows a guy. Hear a need, reach for a name, make the introduction.

Here's the part that changes everything about it. You're not the only one doing it.

Most of us picture networking as a solo sport. Your network is the people *you* know. Your reach is as far as *your* memory and *your* diary stretch. And however well you do it, there's a ceiling built in - one set of eyes and ears, listening out for one person's opportunities.

Now picture it differently. You're part of a circle of people who all build their networks the same way you do. They chose the same method, filter on the same attitude, and hold the same habit of giving introductions away freely. Each of them knows what you do, and each of them is listening out for you.

And behind every one of them sits their wider network. Everyone in that circle has spent years collecting people who know them well and trust their judgement - clients, colleagues, old friends from jobs long gone. When they recommend someone, it lands.

Do the sums. A circle of a hundred people, each with a hundred people like that, is ten thousand people. Every one of them is a single trusted introduction away from you. None of them are cold contacts or names on a list - each one comes with somebody vouching for you on the way in.

That's the real difference between collecting contacts and joining something. A contact list of ten thousand is worth almost nothing - nobody on it is listening for you. Ten thousand people you can reach through someone they trust is a different animal altogether.

But it only works on one condition, and it's the one people skip. You can only recommend someone if you understand what they do - clearly enough to spot the right person the moment they cross your path. A circle full of people who've never actually talked is just a directory with better manners.

So when someone in the circle is introduced to you, take the half hour. Not because there's an obvious business fit - often there isn't - but because afterwards you'll know what they offer and they'll know what you offer. From then on you're both listening across each other's wider networks.

And the circle keeps growing the same way it started. Every so often you'll meet someone - often out of your own wider network - who lights up at all this and wants to build the way you do. Bring them in. The circle gets bigger for everyone, and so does the wider network behind it.

All of which rests, again, on one small act: one person introducing two others. It sounds easy, and most people do it badly. The next piece is about doing it well.
