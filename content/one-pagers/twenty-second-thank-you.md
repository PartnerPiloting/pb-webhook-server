# A thanks-for-connecting that delights

I've got a confession: until recently, I was bulk-sending "thanks for connecting" messages myself.

Me. The bloke writing you a series about doing this properly. Not from laziness - I was simply certain a genuine message for each person would take too long. I suspect that's where most people sit: not careless, just quietly sure the good version can't be done at scale.

The arithmetic backs them up: open the profile, read it properly, find the thing worth saying, write it - ten minutes each, for every new connection, forever. So we all made the same quiet trade: the same line to everyone, or nothing at all.

And everyone can smell it. "Thanks for connecting! I'd love to learn more about your business" - you delete those. So do I. They've just said yes to you, and for about a day you're welcome in their inbox. The standard move is to walk into that moment holding a photocopy.

You don't have to hand-write to everyone - the system can send a simple, decent thanks automatically. That's the floor. For the people worth it, you can do something in a different league.

And it isn't "personalisation" in the usual sense. "Hi Sarah, thanks for connecting!" is a mail-merge wearing a smile. What lands is a genuine insight about *their* work - a specific observation, a real question about what they clearly care about. The effect isn't "they used my name". It's "this person actually looked at me." People are rarely truly seen, and they don't forget the ones who bother.

Then the constraint my habit was built on dissolved. The AI now reads the profile properly and drafts something insightful, in my voice. What's left for me is the twenty seconds that need a human: check it rings true, tweak it if it doesn't, send.

"In my voice" matters most. Not a message that sounds like AI - people smell that too - but one that sounds more like you than you do: you on your best day, every time. That comes from tuning: your tweaks get folded back in, and the drafts keep getting closer to how you speak.

Twenty seconds only pays because of the decisions before it. Connect with whoever the algorithm served up and a delightful thank-you is lipstick on a cold list. But if you chose the room and scored for attitude, this message lands on exactly the kind of person who notices. Often inside the fortnight comes a "sure, worth a quick chat?" you barely had to ask for.

A good one is short. Specific - one real detail from their world, interpreted, never parroted back. Curious - it asks about them. Patient - no pitch, no link, no "grab a time here." You're opening a conversation, not processing a lead.
