The numbers

What it costs, and what it's worth. The second one is the more interesting number.

You asked what it costs. I'll give you the figure in a moment, but it's the less interesting half of the answer - so let me do the other half first.

What you're actually buying

Not software, and not a bigger contact list. A small group of carefully chosen people who look out for one another - and then, as the people in that group start building groups of their own, work beginning to arrive from two and three steps away. From people you have never met, because someone who trusts you vouched for you to someone who trusts them.

That's the thing worth paying for. Not more contacts - a source of work that keeps producing after you've stopped pushing. It's the end of wondering where the next piece is coming from. I've written that argument out properly in Building nodes, and it's the one page I'd read before deciding anything.

What that's worth

Start with what the alternatives charge. There's a whole category of paid networks that will sell you a vetted circle of professionals, and a thousand a month is not unusual. Breakfast clubs run around $3,500 a year. Both hand you a room somebody else assembled, to somebody else's criteria - and the day you stop paying, it's gone. You did the relationship building. They kept the platform.

This costs less than the breakfast club, and a fraction of the paid network. And what you build is yours. It doesn't switch off when you stop paying, because it was never a room - it's a set of relationships with your name on them.

Then there's the simpler test, and it's the one I'd actually apply. One decent client covers the year. Not a big one. One.
