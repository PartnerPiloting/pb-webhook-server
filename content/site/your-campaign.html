One campaign, and the message that matters

One campaign runs on its own and keeps your list filling. Your part is the thank-you - and that's where the meetings come from.

Your Linked Helper machine runs one campaign. Just one. It sends your connection requests, keeps the people who accept, and hands them to Wingguy to be scored. You don't touch any of that.

What you do is thank the people worth thanking. Here's the whole thing, top to bottom.

  2. They're invited to connectWith a short note in your words.
  3. Some acceptAnyone who doesn't simply drops out.

The white boxes happen by themselves. The two at the bottom are yours.

What the campaign does

It's already built when you sign in to your machine. Four steps, one after the other:

  1. Invite. It sends your connection request with a short note. The note starts out as mine, and you make it yours. Keep it an offer, not a pitch.
  2. Check who accepted. Anyone who hasn't accepted simply drops out. Nobody is chased.
  3. Visit and extract. For the ones who accepted, it visits their profile properly - so they see you've looked - and reads their headline, About section and posts. And because they're now a first-level connection, it picks up their email address as well.
  4. Send to your database. They land in Wingguy, in your own list.

We go through it together before it starts

The campaign on your machine is a template, based on the one I run myself. It works - but it's mine. My note, my days, my numbers.

So before it sends anything, you and I go through it together. It takes one sitting, and you come away knowing what every setting does:

  • The invitation note - rewritten in your words, with your name on it.
  • How many go out at a time, and how long it rests between batches.
  • Which days and hours it sends on.
  • Your search - who goes into it.

It can't send anything until it has people in it, so nothing happens before we've done this. The pacing is already set to stay well inside LinkedIn's limits, and once we've set it for you, leave it as it is unless I say otherwise.

Then they're scored

Everyone who arrives is scored against your attributes - the things that tell you whether this is the sort of person the method works with. Scoring runs once a day, so people who accept today have their score tomorrow. Nothing's broken if someone shows up without one.

You're given a set of attributes to begin with. Most people keep them close to as they are.

Where the magic is

Almost nobody sends a properly personal thank-you when someone connects. They send nothing, or the same line to everyone. So the person who does it well stands out straight away.

The reason nobody does it is the effort. You'd have to dig through your recent connections, remember who you'd already thanked, and write each one from scratch. The Thanks for Connecting screen in your portal does the remembering for you:

  • It lists everyone who has accepted and hasn't been thanked yet, with their score beside them.
  • Sort by highest score, and the best people are at the top.
  • Click a name and their LinkedIn opens. Type /wg in the message box and Wingguy reads their profile and writes the message right there.
  • Read it, change what you like, and send it. Mark them Messaged and they leave the list.
  • Work down from the top. When the scores drop off, mark the rest Skipped and don't give them another thought.

Your place is kept, so you can do three today and ten tomorrow.

If you remember the old way

If you've been with me a while, you'll remember we used to do this the other way round. One campaign collected people, they were scored, and then you copied the top scorers across into a second campaign that invited them.

It worked. But it was a job you had to remember every week, with copying and pasting in the middle of it, and the honest truth is that people forgot.

Now nothing has to be remembered. Everyone in your search is invited, and the scoring happens after they accept. The filter hasn't gone - it's moved, from who you invite to who you write to. If the old campaigns are still on your machine, leave them paused.
