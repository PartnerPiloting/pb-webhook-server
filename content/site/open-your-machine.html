Getting into your Linked Helper machine

About ten minutes of your time, and nothing to install. Once you've signed in, the machine finishes setting itself up.

These pictures were taken on 29 and 30 September 2026. If what you're looking at doesn't match, go by what your Claude tells you rather than by the picture.

Your Linked Helper machine lives in a data centre. You look at it through your web browser, from any computer. There's no app to install and nothing to keep running.

Moving across from Linked Helper on your own computer?

Pause the campaigns on your old copy before you start, and don't uninstall it yet.

The machine also brings your history across, so it knows who you've already contacted. You'll see Linked Helper close and open again once while it does that. Let it go.

If your old copy has been busy since you sent me your export, send a fresh one first: put the file in your OneDrive, Google Drive or Dropbox, share it so anyone with the link can view it, then in Claude type put this file on my machine and paste the link.

1Open your link

Ask your Claude. Start a new chat and type open my Linked Helper machine. It hands you your own link - click it.

The link is also in the email I sent you, so you can simply click that. Bookmark it once it's open and you won't need either again.

2Show it's you

The first time, a page asks for your email address. Use the one I have on file for you - the one my emails arrive at. Then click Send login code.

A six-digit code arrives in your inbox, usually within a minute. Type it into the page. If it hasn't turned up, check your junk folder.

It remembers you on that browser for about a month, so most days the link opens straight to your machine. When the month is up it asks for a fresh code. That's normal - nothing is broken.

3Sign in to Linked Helper

Your machine's screen opens in the browser tab, with Linked Helper waiting for you. Give it a few seconds to appear.

4Sign in to LinkedIn

Linked Helper then opens a LinkedIn window. Sign in the way you normally would. LinkedIn hasn't seen this machine before, so it will text a code to your phone - have it beside you.

You only do this once. After that the machine stays signed in.

5Leave it for ten minutes

That's your part done. The machine finishes the job by itself - you don't need to tell me, and there's nothing to click.

Within a few minutes it builds your campaign. When it's finished, a box appears on the screen saying Your Linked Helper machine is set up, and the same note is left on the machine's desktop.

6Give your campaign some people

Your campaign is built, but it has nobody in it yet. In Claude, start a new chat and type Help me with my first campaign. It walks you through putting your search in and making the invitation note sound like you.

From there it runs on its own. Everyone who accepts is scored, and you work through them on the Thanks for Connecting screen in your portal, highest score first.

Coming and going

  • When you've finished looking, just close the tab. Linked Helper keeps running on the machine.
  • Don't close Linked Helper itself, and don't sign out of LinkedIn inside it. If you do by accident, the machine restarts Linked Helper by itself within about five minutes. A LinkedIn sign-out is the one thing it can't fix on its own, so let me know.
  • If I happen to be looking at your machine at the same time, we see the same screen and share the mouse. Nothing breaks - one of us just waits.

If something doesn't work

  • No code in your inbox? Check junk first. Some work email systems block it - tell me and I'll add your personal address as well.
  • It says your email isn't allowed? You've typed a different address from the one I have on file. Try your other one, or tell me which you'd rather use.
  • The page says Reconnecting? In the small hours the machine restarts itself. Leave the tab open and it comes back within a few minutes.
  • Still stuck? Text me on 0414 975 509 with a photo of what you're seeing.
