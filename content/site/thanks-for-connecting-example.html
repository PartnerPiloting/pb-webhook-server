The message that decides whether you ever meet

When someone accepts your connection request, most people say nothing. The rest send the same line to everybody. Nobody has ever written a real one to every new connection, because it took too long.

Here are three new connections, what everyone sends them, and what Wingguy drafted in about twenty seconds each.

Example - made-up people

RT

Rachel Tomlinson

Fractional CFO · Getting founder-led businesses bank-ready

From her About sectionTwelve years in corporate lending before I switched sides of the table.

86

What everyone sends

Thanks for connecting, Rachel! I'd love to learn more about your business. Let me know if you'd like to jump on a call.

Could have gone to anyone. Deleted.

What Wingguy drafted

Hi Rachel - thanks for connecting. Twelve years on the lender's side before switching sides means you know what the bank wants to see well before the founder does. I'm curious what made you jump. Worth a quick Zoom in the next couple of weeks? Guy

One real detail, read properly, and a question about her. No pitch.

What everyone sends

Three people, one message.

What Wingguy drafted

Hi Helen - thanks for connecting. Burnout prevention for law and accounting firms is a brave niche - they're the people least likely to admit they need it. And a Saturday parkrun for 200 might be the best burnout prevention going. How do you get firms to say yes? Happy to compare notes on a quick Zoom. Guy

Two details, connected. It reads like someone who was paying attention.

It keeps your place, too

The other reason nobody does this properly: on LinkedIn you'd have to dig through your recent connections and remember who you'd already thanked. The Thanks for Connecting screen does the remembering. Everyone who's accepted and not yet been thanked, score beside them. Click a name, type /wg in the message box, and the draft is there. Mark them Messaged and they drop off the list.

Why the second one works

  • Short. Four lines, not a letter.
  • Specific. One real thing from their world, understood, not parroted back.
  • Curious. It asks about them. The whole point is to find out what they do.
  • Patient. No pitch, no link, no booking page. Just an easy yes to a conversation.

And it sounds like you

These are written from my instructions. Yours start out the same way, already reading like a person rather than a machine. Then you shape them to how you talk, and Wingguy learns from every change you make to a draft before you send it.

On day one it's good. A few weeks in, it reads like you - and I find it often sounds more like me than I do at the end of a long day.

Twenty seconds each, for every new connection. That's the difference between a list of people you're connected to and a diary full of conversations.
