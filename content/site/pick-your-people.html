Picking your people on LinkedIn - and copying the search link

Choosing who to invite is a different job from topping up the campaign, and it's the one people forget. Here it is, start to finish.

Your campaign invites whoever is in the search you give it. So the search is the decision about who ends up in your network. Spend ten minutes on it. Everything after that runs by itself.

Start with your Claude

Don't work this out alone. Open a chat with your Claude and say help me work out who to search for.

Tell it what you do, who you've already invited, and whether you're on LinkedIn Premium or Sales Navigator. It will talk through who's worth knowing next and then write the search for you.

Two things to keep in mind while you talk it through:

  • It doesn't need to be precise. You're not hunting for clients. You're getting in front of capable people you'd be happy to recommend, and plenty of the best ones will never buy from you.
  • Tell it your plan. A LinkedIn Premium search can only hold five ANDs, ORs and NOTs. Sales Navigator allows fifteen. Claude will stay inside the limit if it knows which one you have.

The steps below are for building the search on LinkedIn once you know who you're after.

First, who?

Write one sentence before you open LinkedIn: "People who ___, in ___, who I'd be happy to recommend." A job title or two, a place, maybe a company size. If you can't write the sentence, the search will be mush and so will the network.

Look for people who already talk to the people you want to reach. That's the angle that pays best. If they're in front of an audience you'd like to be in front of, then when something comes up in your ballpark they can put you forward - and you can do the same for them.

Two things worth adding to the search:

  • Experience. People with real years behind them tend to have real networks behind them.
  • Activity. Favour people who post. It proves they're actually on LinkedIn and will see your request.

The search is about what they can do. Attitude comes later. Once people are in your list, Wingguy scores them on whether they look like someone who's out meeting people and happy to connect others. You can't see that in a LinkedIn search, so don't try.

Don't agonise over it. A search isn't a commitment. You only send about a hundred requests a week, so even a month aimed slightly wrong teaches you how to aim the next one. And good connectors refer people anyway, even if they're not who you pictured.

Then one rule that matters more than any filter: 2nd and 3rd degree connections only. The campaign sends connection requests. You can't send one to somebody you're already connected to, so 1st-degree people would just sit in the queue and never go anywhere.

In Sales Navigator (the better search)

  1. Open Sales Navigator and choose Lead filters (people, not accounts).
  2. Under Relationship, tick 2nd degree and 3rd degree+. Leave 1st unticked.
  3. Add your filters: Current job title, Geography, Company headcount, Industry - whatever your sentence says. Fewer, sharper filters beat many loose ones.
  4. Look at the first page of results. Would you be glad to be connected to most of them? If not, tighten a filter and look again.
  5. When it looks right, click in the address bar at the top of the browser, select the whole address and copy it. That long address is your search link.

Saved searches. If you save the search and copy the link from the saved-search page, the address only carries an ID, not the filters. It still works for a top-up, but Wingguy can't read the degree filter from it - so do that check yourself in step 2.

In plain LinkedIn search

  1. Type your keywords into the search box at the top and press Enter, then click People under the search box.
  2. Click Connections and tick 2nd and 3rd+. Leave 1st unticked.
  3. Add Locations, and under All filters anything else your sentence needs - current company, industry, school.
  4. Check the first page looks like your people.
  5. Copy the whole address from the address bar. It will contain /search/results/people/ - that's the right kind.

How big should it be?

A few hundred people is a good top-up. Linked Helper reads about 95 pages of a search a day (roughly 950 people), so a bigger search simply carries on tomorrow. A search of 10,000 is not better than one of 400 - it's just looser.

Then top up

Paste the link on your top-up page (the link is in your Monday email), or tell your Claude top up my campaign with this search and paste it there. Wingguy does the rest and emails you how many it collected. Anyone already in your list is skipped, so expect fewer than the search shows.
