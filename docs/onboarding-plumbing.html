The two standing rules

One action for me, the rest is Claude's

My half of any step is a single thing I can do without leaving the conversation - usually "paste this into the meeting chat". Minting links, reading account ids out of dashboards, setting Airtable fields, running health checks: Claude's half, silently. If a step reads like it needs me to open a second window, it is written wrong.

Lead with the action, not the architecture

"What's our next step with this client?" gets one action and an offer to do it - not an explanation of how the plumbing works. The explanation is available the moment I ask for it, and never before.

Restate every step from scratch

No "as we discussed", no assumed memory. A dozen client sessions a week: the cost of repeating a step I remember is three seconds; the cost of assuming I remember is a stalled call in front of a client.

The meeting link

One reusable personal link on every invite, automatically. We deliberately do NOT generate a fresh link per meeting - one link that never changes is simpler, can't fail at booking time, and makes back-to-back call days painless.

5 minNever skip
Say to the client

"When Wingguy books a meeting for you, the invite needs a 'click here to join' link. Do you have a personal meeting link? Zoom calls it your Personal Meeting Room. Meet and Teams have the same thing - a link you can reuse forever. If you've never set one up, let's do it right now, it's two minutes.

The beauty of it: one link, on every invite, automatically. Your leads just click and away we go - nothing to generate, nothing to forget, nothing to break. While you're in there we'll switch on the waiting room, so if someone's running late to one call they can't wander into your next one."

Claude does

The meeting recorder

Two moments sell this: "draft an email based on the transcript of the call we just had", and "prep me for my meetings". After those two, they will never want a transcript missed - which is the whole pitch.

Calendar must be done firstBranches by tool
Say to the client

"One more connection, and it's the sleeper hit: your meeting recorder. Here's what it feels like. You finish a call, and you say to Wingguy: 'draft an email based on the transcript of the call we just had' - and watch the job it does. That's not just work off your plate, it's the strain gone too: you don't have to hold the whole call in your head any more.

Then before your next meeting you say 'prep me for my meetings' and it pulls the transcript from last time and clues you in. Once you've felt those two, you won't want a single transcript to ever be missed - and that's why we recommend Granola: it takes notes straight on your computer, no bot joining your calls, and it works whatever the platform - even when you're the guest."

Lane

Why Granola first: it captures on the client's own computer - no bot joins the call. Identical on Zoom, Meet and Teams; works when the client is a guest rather than host; never puts an extra "participant" in the meeting for a lead to wonder about. On our side, the Granola pipe was built per-client from day one - own key, own webhook, own signing secret - so it's the one lane genuinely ready for every client.

I do

  • Paste the key into Granola API Key on their row.
  • Run node scripts/register-granola-webhook.js --client=<Client-ID> as a Render one-off job.
  • It prints a signing secret, shown once - paste into Granola Webhook Secret. The connection is not live until that paste happens.

The client does

  1. Gets set up on granola.ai if they aren't already. The API key needs Granola's Business plan - check before promising anything.
  2. In settings, creates an API key and gives it to me.

Claude does

  • Runs the registration job and confirms it listed cleanly (--list).
  • Matches the meeting to a lead off the client's calendar once a note files.

Check it worked

  • After their next recorded meeting the client asks Wingguy "what was my last meeting?" and sees the note come back.
Traps

The one non-Granola recorder with a real pipe - proven 2026-08-21, Rick Wong first through. It is a bot-joining tool, and Fireflies only fires for meetings the account OWNS - a client who is a guest on someone else's Fireflies call gets nothing. For a client already living in Fireflies it's a straight swap, and their phone app covers face-to-face meetings too.

The client does

  2. Settings → Webhooks. An existing config shows as a row that does NOT open on click - hover it, then three dots → Edit. None there? Add Config.

  • Never send them away hoping. The three-dots menu beside Add Config has Push past meetings: pick a date range and Fireflies pushes real historic meetings through on demand. Watch them file, then tell the client it works.

Traps

Otter, or whatever they already use. The order matters here.

  1. Re-sell the why, once - except Fireflies, which has its own lane. The point was never the brand: it's that no transcript is ever missed. Other tools have no pipe into Wingguy, so their transcripts go nowhere - no draft-from-the-call, no prep-me, no history. And most work by sending a bot into the call, the thing Granola exists to avoid.
  2. If they still insist, don't fight it - stack instead. They keep their tool for whatever they like about it, and run Granola alongside. Granola captures on their computer, so it doesn't clash with a bot tool at all. They give up nothing; Wingguy stays fed.
  3. The escape hatch, framed honestly. The odd transcript can be pasted into chat and Wingguy files it. But walk them through what "manual" means before they build a workflow on it: export it, paste it in, give the lead's email so it links to the right person (no email, no link - the note is invisible next time they ask about that person), and an unlabelled paste needs a confirm step before the summary exists. Granola has done all of that before they've stood up from the call. Offer the paste door as a patch, not a plan.
  4. Never promise a custom pipe for their tool on the call. That's a development decision, not an onboarding one - "I'll look into it" is the strongest commitment to make.

Extension delivery

The extension installs once, but it gets improved - and updates must reach the client's computer with zero ongoing effort from them. One Wingguy folder per client, owned by me in my cloud storage, shared view-only. Their sync tool pulls it down; the browser picks up the new version at next restart.

Wingguy Enabled = Yes onlyAsk → lane → folder → ship → install

The ask - two questions, before anything else

  1. "Is the computer you use for LinkedIn your own, or one your company manages?"
  2. "Do you have a personal Microsoft account?" For Windows users the answer is nearly always yes - it's what they sign into Windows with.

Own machine + personal Microsoft account = OneDrive lane, no IT department in the loop. Company-managed machine = zip lane, or updates together on calls - don't fight the policy.

View-only is load-bearing

The folder's contents execute inside the client's browser. Only my account may ever be able to write to it. Never grant edit rights.

Their row carries Extension Folder Provider and Extension Folder Ref.

The comms doctrine

  • Ask first, then send exactly ONE card that matches their answer. Nobody reads steps that don't apply to them.
  • Cards are frozen canonical text, proven click-for-click on a real machine first.
  • Every step says WHERE it happens (File Explorer, never the browser) and ends with "you'll know it worked when...".
  • Every risky step has a zero-cost exit: "stop here and reply - we'll do it together on the call, two minutes, nothing lost."
  • The loop closes BEFORE the call. No session ever starts with a delivery surprise.
Lane

Why it's the default: a OneDrive folder is a REAL local path that exists from boot, so with the "Always keep on this device" pin the extension folder is always readable, no matter what starts first. Nearly every Windows user already has what's needed - the OneDrive app ships preinstalled and Windows 11 setup already made them a personal Microsoft account. Macs install it from the App Store.

I do

  1. Create OneDrive/Wingguy-clients/<Client-ID>/Wingguy.
  2. Share the inner Wingguy folder to their personal Microsoft address from onedrive.live.com with "Can view". (Windows' right-click "Give access to" is LAN sharing, NOT OneDrive - always share from the website.)
  3. Row: Extension Folder Provider = onedrive, Extension Folder Ref = Wingguy-clients/<Client-ID>/Wingguy.
  4. First ship: node scripts/ship-extension.js --client=<Client-ID> run locally on my machine - table must say OK at the current version. Give OneDrive a minute to sync.
  5. Send the card.

The client's card - two steps, once

  1. Add my folder to your OneDrive. Open the share message and click the link - it opens the OneDrive website. Sign in with the personal Microsoft account. You'll see the Wingguy folder - click Add shortcut to My files.
    You'll know it worked when: the Wingguy folder appears in File Explorer under OneDrive in the left-hand column.

    You'll know it worked when: the icon becomes a solid green circle with a white tick.

How a ship runs

node scripts/ship-extension.js covers every configured client and prints a per-client table. Each lane runs where its access lives, and the script SKIPS - never silently drops - what it can't reach.

  • OneDrive clients: run locally. It copies into my synced folder and the OneDrive app carries them up. No Azure, no tokens, ever. A Render run lists them SKIPPED.
  • Zip clients: no script - build the zip and hand it over.

Traps
Parked 2026-08-25. Do not offer. Do not send the cards. The push code stays dormant in ship-extension.js.

Why it's parked: Google Drive for desktop's streaming mode mounts a virtual drive (G:) only after the Drive app starts - and Chrome silently removes a developer-mode extension whose folder it can't read at browser start. So any reboot where the browser wins the startup race makes the extension vanish. Hit Guy twice; diagnosed 2026-08-25. The only honest client experience on this lane is "sometimes it disappears after a restart, here's the 30-second re-load" - not good enough as a default. Revisit only for a genuinely Microsoft-refusing client, eyes open, or fold it into the Chrome Web Store endgame at ~20+ extension clients.

Traps

Every trap on this page actually happened.

If Followup Brief = Yes but Anthropic API Key is still blank, the overnight brief doesn't fail - it quietly falls back to my platform key, so I silently pay for their nightly run. No alarm fires, because nothing broke.

The fix is just sequence: key in the field first, brief switched on second.

A managed-plan client is the deliberate exception - they are meant to run on my key.

The rules session used to sit at step 2, precisely so that the client's voice was in before anything got drafted. Moving it to step 5 buys a better interview and costs this: between the connector and the rules session, anything Wingguy writes is generic. No sign-off, no offer, no turns of phrase - and none of the "what I'd never say" guardrails.

What that means on the call
  • Don't demo drafting before step 5. Prove the mailbox by asking it to find and read back an email, never to write one. A flat first draft is the one impression that's hard to walk back.
  • Same for the calendar step - "what's on my calendar this week?" reads back, it doesn't compose.
  • If the client asks it to draft something off their own bat, say the honest thing: "that's the next step - it doesn't know how you talk yet."
The other exposure

A client who stalls somewhere around steps 2-4 now has plumbing and no voice on the record at all. Under the old order they'd at least have their instructions. Watch for a client going quiet before step 5 - that's the one to chase.

If a gap opens up: a two-minute mini-version - name, what they do, email sign-off - closes the worst of it without spending the twenty-minute interview early. The full session still happens at step 5.
