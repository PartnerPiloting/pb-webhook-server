# What you just saw

*For the person who watched the demonstration - and the person they'll want to tell about it.*

If we've just had a session and your reaction landed somewhere between "hang on" and "that can't be real" - that's the normal one. People who build AI systems for a living have the same reaction. This page exists because the feeling fades and the argument shouldn't: here's what you watched, again, in plain words - slow enough to forward to someone who wasn't in the room.

## The part everyone remembers

Most days I introduce two people in my network to each other. The whole of what I say is: "Here are two people. Draft the introduction - use my instructions and everything you have." And it has everything: my calls with each of them - the transcripts - the emails, the LinkedIn threads.

What comes back isn't a draft to work on. It's the introduction, done - the full set of emails, each one written to its reader in the terms of their own world: what they're building, why the other person matters to them specifically, what they said to me that made the connection obvious. I read them, and I send them.

Here's the thing about introductions: everyone agrees they're the most valuable move in networking, and almost nobody makes them - because doing one properly means re-reading every note you have on two people and then an hour getting the wording right. That's why they don't happen. Mine happen every day.

## The morning

Before my first call I ask one thing: "Prep me for today's meetings." Back comes each person's story - who they are, what we said last time, what I promised, what I should raise. In a demonstration this morning it briefed me on the very meeting I was in at the time - including a heads-up that my guest and another man I'd spoken with that week carried the same credential and probably knew each other. They did.

And the standing dread anyone with a real network carries - who's owed a reply, who's gone quiet, what did I promise whom - is now one sentence: "Show me my follow-ups." The whole queue, already triaged. Not a system I have to feed and check. A colleague who already checked it.

## The booking

Last week a new connection in Perth suggested we talk. I didn't send a scheduling link - I never do; being offered a few considered times is part of how I work. Wingguy read my calendar, offered him times in *his* timezone, and when he picked one it checked for clashes, confirmed with me, booked it, and built the invite itself - Zoom link, both our LinkedIn profiles, his phone number and email, all gathered from the conversation we'd just had. I never opened my calendar.

## "Sort it"

Two emails arrived overnight from someone juggling a meeting time - calendar reschedules with empty bodies, the kind of thing that eats ten minutes of squinting and cross-checking. My entire instruction was: "Sort it." It read both, worked out his timezone, proposed the answer, and offered to make the booking.

## The messages

The messages you saw it write - the thank-yous, the replies, the outreach - are drafted from everything it holds: the thread, the person's profile, our whole history. Nothing is ever sent without me reading it. And the honest description isn't "better than I'd write" - it's what I would have written with twenty minutes and total recall, delivered in about twenty seconds. When I do want something different, I tell it once - "from now on, do it this way" - and that becomes one of its standing instructions. It writes more like me every week.

And yes - a message that well-informed often reads like AI helped write it. I don't hide that. It gets things right *because* it has the transcripts and the emails and everything in between - and what it writes is what I meant.

## The numbers

I'll keep this modest, because it's one campaign and your field may differ. Over the past month, of the connection requests my system sent to people it had scored highly, about six in ten connected. If you've ever done cold outreach, you know what your own number was. The difference isn't clever wording - it's selection: reaching out only to people scored on attitude, not job title.

## What's left for me

The noticing, the generosity, the conversations - still mine, still the point. The remembering, the tracking, the drafting, the chasing, the diary work - no longer my job. That's the whole trick. I only do the part that needed a human, which is why I can do so much more of it.
